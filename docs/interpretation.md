# Scientific Interpretation Guide

This guide explains how to properly interpret the numerical, statistical, and visual outputs generated by **ConformAtlas**.

---

## 1. Principal Components ($PC_1$ and $PC_2$)

Principal Component Analysis (PCA) performs a linear dimensionality reduction on atomic displacements from the mean structure:
* **$PC_1$**: The eigenvector corresponding to the largest eigenvalue ($\lambda_1$). It captures the direction of maximal conformational variance in the Cartesian coordinate space.
* **$PC_2$**: The second largest orthogonal mode ($\lambda_2$), representing the dominant motion uncorrelated with $PC_1$.
* **Cumulative Explained Variance**: If the first two modes capture $>40\text{--}60\%$ of total variance, projection onto $(PC_1, PC_2)$ typically provides a meaningful representation of major large-amplitude domain motions (e.g. hinge bending, subunit twisting). If the variance is dispersed across hundreds of small modes, 2D projections may obscure higher-dimensional transitions.

---

## 2. Free Energy Landscape (FEL) Minima and Barriers

$$\Delta G(x, y) = -R \cdot T \cdot \ln\left[ \frac{P(x, y)}{P_{\max}} \right]$$

* **$\Delta G = 0\ \text{kJ/mol}$ (Global Minimum)**: The state or conformation with the highest observed probability density.
* **Local Minima (Conformational Basins)**: Metastable conformational states separated by free energy barriers.
* **Energy Ridges / Saddles**: Regions of lower probability separating basins. The difference in free energy between a saddle point and a basin minimum represents the effective barrier height along the chosen projection coordinates.
* **Unobserved Regions (Grey / NaN)**: Regions of coordinate space where zero frames were observed. These are strictly masked as $\text{NaN}$ and must not be confused with zero-energy states.

> [!WARNING]
> **Thermodynamic Equivalence Warning**:
> A low-energy basin on a Cartesian PCA landscape does **not** automatically correspond to a distinct biochemical functional state.
> Furthermore, Boltzmann inversion assumes that the simulation trajectory represents an unbiased, properly sampled equilibrium distribution.
> For biased simulations (Metadynamics, Umbrella Sampling), reweighting must be applied before interpreting the surface as an equilibrium free energy.

---

## 3. Conformational State Populations & Watershed Basins

* **Watershed Segmentation**: Rather than arbitrarily thresholding individual pixels, ConformAtlas uses topological watershed segmentation. Catchment basins naturally group together all frames in the basin's valley.
* **Major vs Minor States**:
  - **Major States (`State A`, `State B`, ...)**: Basins accounting for $\ge 5\%$ (default, configurable via `--min-state-population`) of the total ensemble. Ordered by population: State A is the most populated.
  - **Minor / Transitional States**: Low-occupancy basins ($< 5\%$) representing transient unfolding, intermediate barrier crossings, or sparsely sampled regions.
* **Conservation**: The sum of all major states plus unassigned/minor conformations strictly equals $100\%$.

## 4. Sampling & Convergence Diagnostics

### 4.1 Subspace Overlap (RMSIP)
The Root Mean Square Inner Product compares the dominant PCA subspace between the first and second halves of the trajectory:
* **$\text{RMSIP} \ge 0.70$**: Good evidence of subspace stability. The principal directions of motion remain consistent between trajectory halves.
* **$0.50 \le \text{RMSIP} < 0.70$**: Moderate / mixed stability. Further sampling is recommended.
* **$\text{RMSIP} < 0.50$**: Subspace has not stabilized; motions in the second half are exploring orthogonal directions.

### 4.2 Cosine Content ($c_1$)
Cosine content tests whether motion along $PC_1$ resembles random Brownian diffusion (Hess, 2000):
* **$c_1 \ge 0.70$**: Motion strongly resembles a half-cosine wave. This is a hallmark of diffusive drift / non-converged random walk along the principal mode.
* **$c_1 \le 0.50$**: Motion fluctuates around an equilibrium or undergoes discrete transitions between basins rather than simple linear drift.

### 4.3 Sampling Assessment Heuristics
ConformAtlas reports sampling evidence as:
1. `Good evidence of stability`: High RMSIP ($\ge 0.70$) combined with non-diffusive cosine content ($< 0.50$).
2. `Mixed evidence`: Moderate overlap or borderline diffusion metrics.
3. `Insufficient evidence`: Low overlap ($< 0.50$) or high cosine content ($\ge 0.70$).

These thresholds are empirical heuristics and should be interpreted alongside physical knowledge of the biomolecular system.

---

## 5. Replicate Uncertainty

* **Between-Replicate Error**: When multiple independent simulations are provided, variance across replicates reflects genuine sampling variability.
* **Intra-Trajectory Block Error**: In a single trajectory, adjacent frames are temporally correlated. Block averaging partitions the trajectory to estimate within-trajectory drift, but **cannot** substitute for independent replicates.
