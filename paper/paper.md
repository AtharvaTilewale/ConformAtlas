# Summary

Molecular dynamics (MD) simulations of biomolecules produce trajectories of
thousands to millions of frames. A standard way to summarise such an ensemble is to
project it onto its leading principal components (essential dynamics,
@Amadei1993), histogram the projections, and convert the histogram into a
free-energy landscape by Boltzmann inversion, $\Delta G = -k_\mathrm{B} T \ln
(P/P_\max)$ [@Papaleo2009]. The minima of the landscape are then read as
conformational states, their depths as relative stabilities and the ridges between
them as barriers. The procedure is routine, but nearly every step hides a decision
that changes the answer — which atoms are superposed, how the grid is chosen, what
happens to bins that were never sampled, whether the two coordinates carry enough of
the motion, how many statistically independent frames the trajectory actually
contains — and these decisions are rarely reported, let alone their uncertainty.

ConformAtlas is a Python package and command-line tool that performs this analysis
end to end and reports every one of those decisions together with a statistical
uncertainty. From one or more trajectories (replicates) of a system it computes the
least-squares superposition [@Kabsch1976], a Cartesian, dihedral [@Mu2005;
@Altis2007; @Sittel2014] or time-lagged independent component (TICA, @PerezHernandez2013)
decomposition, the landscape on an automatically or explicitly chosen grid
[@Freedman1981], a topological (watershed) segmentation into states with saddle-point
barriers, block-bootstrap confidence intervals [@Kunsch1989] for populations, state
free energies, barriers and every bin of the landscape, lag-1 transition statistics
and mean first-passage times, representative and average structures per state, and
the convergence diagnostics of the field — subspace overlap [@Amadei1999], cosine
content [@Hess2000; @Hess2002], statistical inefficiency and effective sample size
with automated equilibration detection [@Chodera2007; @Chodera2016]. A second
command projects several systems (for example wild type and mutant) onto one shared
basis and tests population differences with a within-replicate block bootstrap and
an across-replicate Welch's $t$-test, reporting both because they answer different
questions [@Knapp2018; @Grossfield2009]. Results are written as CSV/NumPy tables, PDB
structures, NMD mode files and a self-contained HTML report; every run records its
software versions and options in `run_metadata.json`.

The defining feature of the package is its validation suite. Each numerical
component is checked, in the repository and on every release, against an analytical
result on a synthetic system with a known answer or against an independent
implementation on identical input — MDAnalysis [@MichaudAgrawal2011; @Gowers2016],
GROMACS `covar`, `anaeig`, `sham` and `gyrate` [@Abraham2015], pymbar [@Shirts2008],
deeptime [@Hoffmann2022], SciPy [@Virtanen2020] and ProDy [@Bakan2011]. The report
generated by the suite lists the reference and tolerance of every check, and the
documentation states explicitly which quantities are validated and which are
heuristic conventions.

# Statement of need

PCA-based free-energy landscapes are among the most frequently reported analyses in
the biomolecular simulation literature, and the standard tooling for them is a chain
of GROMACS utilities (`gmx covar`, `gmx anaeig`, `gmx sham`) [@Hess2008;
@Abraham2015] followed by hand-written scripts and manual inspection of a contour
plot. This workflow has well-known weaknesses that the tools themselves do not
flag. `gmx sham` assigns the maximum sampled free energy to bins that were never
visited and silently uses 298.15 K unless told otherwise; the bin count is a fixed
default; the fraction of variance captured by the plotted plane is not shown on the
plot; states are identified by eye; populations are reported without error bars;
and a single trajectory of a few tens of nanoseconds is analysed as if its frames
were independent samples, although the leading principal component of such a
trajectory typically resembles random diffusion [@Hess2000] and the number of
statistically independent frames may be in the single digits. The result is a
literature in which landscape figures from unconverged simulations are compared
across mutants and conclusions are drawn from population differences whose
uncertainty was never estimated [@Grossfield2009; @Knapp2018].

General-purpose trajectory libraries — MDAnalysis [@MichaudAgrawal2011], MDTraj
[@McGibbon2015], ProDy [@Bakan2011] — provide excellent building blocks (PCA,
RMSD, alignment) but leave landscape construction, state definition, uncertainty
estimation and convergence assessment to the user. Markov-state-model packages such
as PyEMMA [@Scherer2015] and deeptime [@Hoffmann2022] address kinetics rigorously
[@Prinz2011] but assume the extensive, well-converged sampling that MSM construction
requires, and are not designed for the common situation of a handful of
replicates of a protein–ligand complex. Reweighting estimators for enhanced-sampling
simulations [@Tiwary2015] solve a different problem and presuppose a bias
potential. ConformAtlas fills the gap between these: it is a complete, validated
implementation of the conventional unbiased-landscape analysis that makes the
hidden decisions explicit, attaches uncertainties to every reported number, and
tells the user, in the report, when the data do not support the conclusion the
figure invites — for instance by refusing a "converged" verdict when fewer than
50 effective frames are present.

# Design and functionality

ConformAtlas is organised as a library (`conformatlas.pipeline.run_analysis`,
`conformatlas.comparison.run_condition_comparison`) with thin Click command-line
wrappers, `conformatlas analyze` and `conformatlas compare`, both of which accept a
YAML configuration. Trajectories are streamed with MDAnalysis and superposed frame
by frame on a user-defined fit group (GROMACS index files are supported); the
covariance eigenproblem is solved in coordinate space or, when $3N$ exceeds the
number of frames, in frame space (dual SVD route). An alternative backend drives
`gmx covar`/`gmx anaeig` for users who require GROMACS numerics. The landscape is
computed with NumPy [@Harris2020] histograms, optionally with per-frame weights for
reweighted ensembles; basins are segmented with the watershed transform of
scikit-image [@vanderWalt2014] on the free-energy surface, sub-basins separated by a
barrier below a threshold (default $1\,k_\mathrm{B}T$) are merged, and unsampled
bins remain undefined rather than being filled. Uncertainties come from a circular
block bootstrap within each replicate whose block length is set from the measured
statistical inefficiency [@Flyvbjerg1989; @Chodera2007]. Figures are produced with
Matplotlib; structures and mode files are written for VMD/NMWiz and ProDy.

# Validation

The validation battery (`validation/validate.py`, report in
`validation/VALIDATION.md`) comprises 147 checks grouped by component. Representative
results: the Boltzmann-inverted landscape of a 2-D Gaussian sample agrees with the
exact bin-integrated free energy to within the expected counting noise (RMSE
0.166 kJ/mol against 0.141 kJ/mol expected); the leading PCA eigenvalues agree with
MDAnalysis to a relative difference below $10^{-7}$ and with `gmx covar` to within
XTC precision; the Kabsch superposition reproduces MDAnalysis's QCP RMSD to
$10^{-5}$ Å; the statistical inefficiency and equilibration time agree with pymbar;
basin populations and saddle barriers of an analytic double-well potential agree
with the Boltzmann integral and the potential; the 95 % block-bootstrap interval
covers the true population in 91.7 % of 120 independently generated correlated
Markov systems, whereas an i.i.d. bootstrap covers in only 30 %; transition
matrices, dwell times and mean first-passage times agree with the generating Markov
chain; TICA eigenvalues and timescales agree with AR(1) theory and with deeptime;
Jensen–Shannon and Bhattacharyya measures agree with SciPy to $10^{-12}$; and the
population-difference test has a type-I error of 0.09 at a nominal 0.05 on
two-plus-two correlated replicates, a mild anti-conservatism that is documented.
The landscape agrees with `gmx sham` bin-for-bin on identical input (median
$|\Delta|$ 0.006 kJ/mol), and the two deliberate differences — no default
temperature, undefined rather than filled unsampled bins — are recorded as checks so
that they remain intentional. The documentation lists the remaining heuristics (the
population and barrier thresholds that define a "major state", the sampling-verdict
thresholds, the automatic grid choice and the lag-1 Markov assumption) as such.
